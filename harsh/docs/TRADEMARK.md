## What you may do without asking

- Use Harsh to build anything, including proprietary and commercial software. The MPL places no obligation on code you write in Harsh, nor on the Rust it generates.
- Say that your software is "written in Harsh", "compatible with Harsh", or "for Harsh".
- Write and publish tutorials, articles, books and courses about Harsh, and use the name in their titles.
- Distribute unmodified copies of the project under its own name.
- Fork the project, modify it, and distribute your fork — **under a different name**.

## What requires permission

- Naming a fork, derivative or modified distribution "Harsh", or a name likely to be confused with it.
- Using the name or logo in a way that suggests the project endorses, sponsors or maintains your product.
- Using the name for a company, product or service whose primary identity is Harsh.

Ask, and permission is usually given. The point of this policy is not to restrict use; it is to keep the name pointing at one thing, so that a person who reads "Harsh" knows which language they are getting.

## Why this exists

The MPL guarantees anyone may fork the code. That is intentional and will not change. What it cannot guarantee is that a person searching for Harsh finds the language described in this repository rather than an incompatible dialect wearing the same name.

Reserving the name is the mechanism that keeps those two things separate. It is the same arrangement Rust itself uses: permissively licensed code, governed name.
